Traditional IT Technologies

Traditional IT focuses on establishing and managing on-premises infrastructure, forming the core technological framework of an organization. Unlike cloud-based solutions, which depend on third-party hosting services, traditional IT provides businesses with direct control over their digital environment. Companies utilizing traditional IT maintain their own email servers, file storage systems, and business applications within physical locations.

This approach offers greater flexibility, enabling businesses to customize their configurations to meet specific operational requirements. By managing their infrastructure in-house, businesses can optimize performance, enhance security, and tailor solutions to suit their unique needs.

Key Roles in IT Infrastructure

The same six jobs, dealt one card at a time. Every layer of the stack has a person behind it — and a room, a rack or a screen where that work actually happens.

01

01of six

System Administrator

Maintains servers, systems, and access for stability and performance.

Owns the machines everything else runs on — provisioning, patching, monitoring and access control. When a box has to stay up through a release, a migration, or a bad Tuesday, this is the person keeping it there.

  • Provisioning
  • Patching
  • Access control
  • Monitoring
02

02of six

Network Administrator

Manages network setup, security, and continuous connectivity.

Designs and runs the paths between everything: switching, routing, firewalls, VPNs, and the wireless nobody thinks about until it drops. The whole job is measured in the minutes nobody noticed.

  • Routing
  • Switching
  • Firewalls
  • VPN
03

03of six

Database Administrator

Manages database design, tuning, backup, and security.

Looks after the data itself — schema design, index and query tuning, replication, and the backups that only matter on the one day they are needed. Also the person who can tell you why that report got slow.

  • Schema design
  • Tuning
  • Replication
  • Backups
04

04of six

IT Support Specialist

Supports users by resolving hardware, software, connectivity issues.

First line for everyone else in the building: hardware, software, accounts and connectivity, triaged and resolved. Most of an organisation’s experience of IT is this role, which is why response time is the number that counts.

  • Triage
  • Hardware
  • Onboarding
  • SLAs
05

05of six

Security Analyst

Implements cybersecurity to protect systems from threats.

Watches for what should not be happening and closes the gap before it does: monitoring, hardening, incident response, and the audits that keep it honest. Prevention is invisible work — this role is how it gets measured.

  • Monitoring
  • Hardening
  • Incident response
  • Audits
06

06of six

Data Center Technician

Maintains hardware, power, and cabling in data centers.

Hands on the physical layer: racking and stacking, structured cabling, power distribution, cooling, and the swap-outs that happen at three in the morning. Every abstraction above depends on this floor staying tidy.

  • Racking
  • Cabling
  • Power
  • Cooling
